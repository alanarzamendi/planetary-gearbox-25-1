01Kinematics and assembly conditions

Architecture: fixed ring, sun input and carrier output in both stages, three planets per stage. The Willis equation relates the speeds of the three members as seen from the carrier:

\[ \frac{\omega_s-\omega_c}{\omega_r-\omega_c} = -\frac{z_r}{z_s} \quad\xrightarrow{\;\omega_r=0\;}\quad i=\frac{\omega_s}{\omega_c}=1+\frac{z_r}{z_s}=1+\frac{72}{18}=5 \]
\[ i_{tot}=i_1\, i_2 = 5\cdot 5 = 25 \qquad n_2=\frac{3000}{25}=120\ \text{rpm} \]

Planet speed, absolute and relative to the carrier. The relative speed is what the teeth and the planet bearing see:

\[ \omega_p = \omega_c\left(1-\frac{z_r}{z_p}\right)=-\tfrac{5}{3}\,\omega_c \qquad n_{p,rel}=(n_s-n_c)\,\frac{z_s}{z_p} \]

Interference detection on all ten gears was run at the assembled pose and again after turning the input 7.3°, which is not a multiple of any tooth pitch. Every mesh engages. The largest overlap is 0.003 cm³, on the stage 1 ring and planet 1. That is a sliver of about 0.2 mm² across the 15 mm face width, or a few hundredths of a millimetre on the flank. 18CrNiMo7-6, carburised and hardened to 58–62 HRC, ground after heat treatment. Through-hardened steel gives \(S_H = 1.07\) on the stage 2 sun. The same design is repeated in stage 1, so every external gear shares one material and one process.

42CrMo4 quenched and tempered to ~300 HB: \(S_H = 2.42\) in stage 2. The internal contact is concave–convex and its pressure is less than half of the sun's. The teeth are cut after tempering, so there is no heat-treatment distortion; nitriding would add cost for no gain.

100Cr6 hardened to 60 HRC and ground: a drawn-cup needle bearing has no inner ring, so the pin is the raceway. Its catalogue rating requires at least 58 HRC.

09Gearbox torque rating

For each criterion, the rated output torque \(T_{2N}\) that brings it exactly to its minimum is solved for, with \(K_A = 1.25\) and 10 000 h. The lowest value is the gearbox rating.

\[ T_{2N,adm}\big|_{L_{10h}} = T_{2N}\left(\frac{L_{10h}}{10\,000}\right)^{3/10}=59.8\cdot\left(\frac{39\,800}{10\,000}\right)^{0.3}\approx 90\ \text{N·m} \]

The contact bars use the assumed \(K_{H\beta} = 1.15\). With \(K_{H\beta}\) from the misalignment budget instead (section 16: M6 pin fit, flanks without crowning), stage 2 contact allows 113 N·m. That is still above 90, so the bearing still governs.

D-010 · Rating limited by the planet bearing

The gearbox is rated at about 90 N·m for 10 000 h, 1.5 times the motor's rated torque. The teeth would carry twice that; the limit is the bearing that fits inside a 40.5 mm planet. If more life were needed, the right move is a wider bearing (HK1216) or two in parallel, not a larger module.

D-011 · Two identical stages

Stage 1 runs at one fifth of the torque (\(S_H = 4.6\)) and could be narrower, but both stages keep \(m = 1.5\) and \(b = 15\). That gives one design of planet, pin, bearing and ring: half the part numbers, one set of tooling and no mix-ups at assembly.

D-014 · Rated output loads

\(F_{2r} = 3.0\) kN radial at mid-length of the shaft extension and \(F_{2a} = 1.5\) kN axial. That is 22 % below the governing limit of 3.86 kN, and gives 23 000 h, more than twice the life requirement, at \(K_A = 1.25\). D-015 · Tapered roller bearings in O arrangement

The O arrangement moves the pressure centres outward, adding 12 mm of effective spread and therefore tilting stiffness under overhung loads. It carries axial load in both directions, and a shaft locknut sets a light preload that removes running clearance, which matters for servo positioning. A pair of deep-groove ball bearings was rejected: lower tilting stiffness and no adjustable preload.

A parallel key has side clearance, which shows up as backlash in a servo axis, and its keyway is a notch with \(\beta \approx 2\)–2.3. 12Input side

Input shaft Ø12, integral with the stage 1 sun. The sun root diameter is 1.94 times the shaft diameter, so the sun body is never weakened by the shaft.

\[ \tau_{max}=\frac{16\,T_{max}}{\pi d^3}=\frac{16\cdot8360}{\pi\cdot12^3}=24.6\ \text{MPa} \quad\Rightarrow\quad S=\frac{R_e}{\sqrt3\,\tau}=17.6 \]

Three planets at 120° cancel each other's mesh forces on the sun, \(\sum \vec F = 0\) in the ideal case. The input bearings therefore carry only the coupling's restoring force and the shaft's weight, generously taken as 200 N per bearing:

\[ L_{10h}=\left(\frac{C_r}{K_A F_r}\right)^{3}\frac{10^6}{60\,n_1}=\left(\frac{7280}{250}\right)^3\frac{10^6}{60\cdot3000}=137\,000\ \text{h} \qquad v_{seal}=\frac{\pi\,d\,n_{max}}{60\,000}=3.77\ \text{m/s} \]
D-017 · Input bearings and coupling

Two 6201-2Z deep-groove ball bearings, chosen by shaft size, since load does not size them. Shields rather than contact seals keep friction low at 6000 rpm; a separate lip seal closes the gearbox, at a surface speed well within the usual limit for elastomer seals. The motor connects through a metal bellows coupling, torsionally stiff and free of backlash, rated at \(T_{KN} \ge 1.5\,T_{max} = 12.5\) N·m, with hub bores Ø19 (motor, confirmed) and Ø12 (gearbox).

The coupling also protects the motor: the force it puts on the motor shaft must stay below the motor's allowable radial load. With the 200 N design assumption the margin is \(392/200 = 1.96\), and a bellows coupling's lateral stiffness keeps the real force far lower for the misalignment a machined adapter flange allows.

14Interstage connection

The stage 1 carrier drives the stage 2 sun. The sun is a separate shaft-gear with an integral spline stub, pressed into a hub on the stage 1 rear carrier plate. The torque it carries is the stage 1 output:

\[ T_{s2}=i_1\,T_{in}: \qquad 5\cdot2.39=11.95\ \text{N·m (rated)} \qquad 5\cdot8.36=41.8\ \text{N·m (peak)} \]

Flank pressure of an involute spline (DIN 5480 W 16 × 1 × 14, engaged length \(L = 8\) mm, load-sharing factor \(\varphi = 0.75\), bearing depth \(h_w \approx 0.9m\)):

\[ p=\frac{2T}{d\,h_w\,L\,z\,\varphi} \qquad p_{rated}=28\ \text{MPa}\ \left(S=\tfrac{100}{28}=3.5\right) \qquad p_{peak}=79\ \text{MPa}\ \left(S=\tfrac{200}{79}=2.5\right) \]
\[ \tau_{peak}=\frac{16\,T}{\pi d_f^3}=\frac{16\cdot41\,800}{\pi\cdot13.8^3}=81\ \text{MPa} \quad\Rightarrow\quad S=\frac{R_e}{\sqrt3\,\tau}=\frac{800}{\sqrt3\cdot81}=5.7 \]
D-020 · Splined interstage, not one integral part

A single part (carrier plate plus sun) would save one joint. But its sun teeth would sit next to a Ø96 flange that blocks the grinding wheel, and the whole carrier would have to be carburised with the sun. As two parts, the sun is ground as a plain shaft-gear and case-hardened on its own, the carrier stays in 42CrMo4 +QT, and each stage can be assembled and run separately. An interference spline fit removes the spline's backlash. A Ø20 shoulder, below the sun root diameter of 23.25 mm, gives the axial stop and the grinding run-out. The intermediate member floats radially on six planets, which also helps load sharing.

15Carrier cages

Each carrier is a cage: one plate carries three bridges between the planets, and the opposite plate is screwed to them (3 × M5). The far plate passes half of every pin force back to the output plate through the bridges. They are modelled as guided beams between the plates, with a 16 mm gap and a 10 × 18 mm section:

\[ V=\frac{T_c/2}{r\,N} \qquad M=\frac{V\,l}{2} \qquad \sigma=\frac{M}{h\,t^2/6} \qquad \text{stage 2 peak: } V=1032\ \text{N},\ \sigma=27.5\ \text{MPa} \]

The bridges must clear the spinning planets. A bridge centred mid-way between two planets, at the planet-centre radius, has its nearest planet centre at \(u=a\cos(\pi/N)-a\) radially and \(w=a\sin(\pi/N)\) tangentially:

D-027 · Housings in EN-GJS-500-7

Input and output housings in ductile iron. Its expansion (11.5 µm/(m·K)) matches the steel rings and bearings, so the dowels and the J7 bearing fits of D-018 stay valid hot. The planet pins are not compared. Study A spreads the pin load along the whole pin, including the lengths inside the plate bores, which understates pin bending (31 MPa against 81 MPa by hand). The hand calculation in section 08, with the load on the 12 mm needle track, stays the design value.

D-032 · FEA checks the design; the hand calculations stay its basis

The acceptance criterion is Sy ≥ 1.5 on the nodal peak at peak load, ignoring singular points at the edges of fixtures and loads. All three studies pass with no geometry change. The lowest value, 1.5, is local: it is at the exit of a tie-bolt tapped hole under the upper-bound preload, where the thread load is modelled as a uniform traction on a smooth hole that meets the restrained face. That joint is governed by the VDI 2230 thread-engagement check (10.5 mm in GJS, section 17). The nominal wall stress there gives Sy = 2.3. The other peaks sit at internal corners that the CAD leaves sharp (MB6 slot, bridge roots, flange–neck corner), so the real filleted parts will be lower.

Mesh friction losses with Ohlendorf's loss factor, \(\mu = 0.05\), and the approach/recess split assumed equal:

\[ H_v=\frac{\pi(u\pm1)}{z_1\,u}\left(1-\varepsilon_\alpha+\varepsilon_1^2+\varepsilon_2^2\right) \qquad \eta_z = 1-\mu H_v \]

23Assumptions and limits of the calculation

  • Sections 05, 06 and 09 use an assumed \(K_{H\beta} = 1.15\). Section 16 replaces it with ISO 6336-1 method C. For these lightly loaded, uncrowned flanks that gives \(K_{H\beta} = 2.77\) and a lowest \(S_H\) of 1.41 instead of 2.18. The 1.15 holds only together with the helix crowning set in D-025. Mesh stiffness \(c_\gamma = 20\) N/(mm·µm) is the ISO typical value for solid steel gears.
  • Planet thrust is taken as 10 % of the tangential force. Straight spur teeth make none in theory, so this covers misalignment and friction.
  • \(K_\gamma = 1.16\) comes from AGMA 6123, because ISO 6336 gives no tabulated value.
  • Ring bending is bounded with a rack; a real internal tooth has a thicker root.
  • Bearing ratings are the lowest found across manufacturers, so any supplier qualifies: HK1212 \(C_r = 6.27\) kN (INA lists 6.5), 32006 X \(C_r = 35.8\) kN (SKF lists 43.9, with the same \(e, Y, Y_0\)), 6201 \(C_r = 7.28\) kN.
  • Shaft fatigue uses estimated notch factors \(\beta\) and a Goodman line, not a full DIN 743 calculation; the 2.0 minimum covers that.
  • The 200 N input bearing load is an assumption for the coupling's restoring force.
  • FEA (section 19) is linear-elastic. The carrier joints are bonded and the ring is modelled without teeth. The SOLIDWORKS library gives EN-GJS-500-7 an elastic modulus of 120 GPa, against 169 GPa in EN 1563, so the housing deflections are overstated by about 40 %; the stresses in its statically determinate load path are not affected.
  • Housing joints: VDI 2230 table values for M6 / M8 preload (\(\mu_G = 0.12\)), \(\alpha_A = 1.6\), \(\mu_T = 0.15\) for dry machined steel and cast-iron faces, and \(p_G = 750\) MPa for EN-GJS-500-7. The SolidWorks library lists \(E = 120\) GPa for this grade; the calculation uses 169 GPa (EN 1563).
  • Catalogue parts are modelled as envelopes with the catalogue boundary dimensions: 32006 X 30 × 55 × 17 with a 13 mm cup, KM 6 Ø45 × 7 with 4 slots 5 × 2, and MB 6 Ø30 / Ø49 × 1. The 32006 X housing abutment (Da 47–49 mm) comes from a distributor listing of an equivalent bearing. The MB 6 groove bottom (M = 27.5 mm, groove 6 mm wide) must be checked against the supplier's drawing.
  • Motor interface, confirmed in the Yaskawa Sigma-7 200 V catalogue (SGM7J-08A): shaft S = Ø19 h6 × LR 40, spigot LB = Ø70 h7 × LE 3, flange LC 80 × LG 8, 4 × Ø7 on LA = Ø90, LL 97 mm, 2.2 kg without brake. With the 8 mm extra of a batteryless encoder or a holding brake, only the motor's own length changes; the interface does not.
  • The spline is modelled in CAD by its major diameter; the DIN 5480 callout goes on the drawing. 100Cr6 is not in the SolidWorks library, so the pins use a project material, 1.3505 (100Cr6) hardened 60 HRC: \(E = 210\) GPa, \(\nu = 0.30\), \(\rho = 7810\) kg/m³, \(R_{p0.2} = 1650\) MPa (the value used in the pin check), \(R_m = 2200\) MPa. Thermal properties are typical handbook values for bearing steel.
